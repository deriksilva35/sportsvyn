# Sportsvyn Preseason Editions, Gridiron 2026

**Edition 0. Seeded by hand. Our poll, not the wire's.**

Four lists. Each is a stated editorial position taken before a snap, which is the only honest thing a preseason ranking can be. Movement computes from here at Week 1.

**Call now made:** dark horses take ranks 16 to 20 with a render rule that breaks them out under a DARK HORSES band. Cheaper than a sibling `ranking_list` and it survives the movement math, because a dark horse that climbs is just a row moving up. The NFL board below runs 1 to 32 and the CFB board runs 1 to 25, with 16 to 20 carrying the band on both.

**Sites sources for Edition 1 onward:** FPI plus AP for CFB, FPI plus the consensus power-ranking mean for NFL. Add to `/methodology` with the CFBD attribution when CFB ships.

# 1. NFL Power Rankings

## Top 15

**1. Los Angeles Rams.** Stafford led the league with 4,707 yards and 46 touchdowns at 37, and then the roster added Myles Garrett on June 1. One trade moved the market from +800 to +500.

**2. Seattle Seahawks.** They finished first in both total and defensive DVOA and won it, which five teams in history have done. Kenneth Walker, Boye Mafe and Riq Woolen are all gone.

**3. Buffalo Bills.** Allen was the only player in football with 3,000 passing yards and 500 rushing yards, and Buffalo still finished two games behind New England in the division.

**4. Baltimore Ravens.** A defense that produced six sacks in its first five games added Trey Hendrickson, and the staff around Jackson and Henry is almost entirely new.

**5. Denver Broncos.** They lost the AFC Championship Game at home with Jarrett Stidham taking the snaps after Bo Nix hurt his ankle against Buffalo. Jaylen Waddle arrives for an offense that had no vertical element.

**6. San Francisco 49ers.** FPI has three NFC West teams in its preseason top five, the first division to manage that since 2022. San Francisco is the one where the argument is age at premium positions.

**7. New England Patriots.** They led the league in offensive EPA per play against the softest schedule in football, then drew the Chargers, the Texans and a Nix-less Denver in January. FPI has them 14th.

**8. Kansas City Chiefs.** Mahomes tore his ACL in Week 15. This rank is a rehabilitation timeline more than it is a roster read, and the roster read is fine.

**9. Detroit Lions.** Goff put up 4,564 yards, 34 touchdowns and eight interceptions behind the same line, and Gibbs is the shortest-priced non-quarterback in the MVP market.

**10. Philadelphia Eagles.** Eleven wins with the worst version of this offense anyone has seen. Sean Mannion takes over the play calling and the personnel around him barely moved.

**11. Houston Texans.** The defense has carried this roster for two seasons. Stroud is the variable the ranking cannot settle before September.

**12. Dallas Cowboys.** Prescott is the first NFC name on the MVP board. The NFC East has not produced a repeat winner in two decades, which is the whole reason this rank is not higher.

**13. Los Angeles Chargers.** Herbert's ceiling has always been a line question. Joe Alt's availability is the single input that moves this number most.

**14. Chicago Bears.** Williams threw 27 touchdowns against seven interceptions in year two. The supporting cast is the same one that produced it.

**15. Green Bay Packers.** Love sits inside the market's top ten again on a roster that returns intact. The ranking is where the roster is, not where the price is.

## Five dark horses

**Cincinnati Bengals.** Burrow played Week 1, Week 2, and then nothing until Week 13. The roster ranked 17th is the roster that had no quarterback for ten games.

**Jacksonville Jaguars.** Lawrence sits inside the MVP market's top ten while Jacksonville sits 16th in the consensus. One of those two numbers is wrong.

**Washington Commanders.** The head coach lost his entire staff and kept his job. Defensive improvement is the only lever available, which also makes it the largest one.

**Tampa Bay Buccaneers.** Every NFC South team went 3-3 in division play and none finished above .500. The price of entry to a division title is lower here than anywhere in football.

**Minnesota Vikings.** O'Connell has produced winning seasons with more than one quarterback. A 19th-ranked roster is not a 19th-ranked coaching staff.

## Ranks 21 to 32

The five above occupy 16 to 20. Reads thin out here by design; a preseason board owes its argument to the teams it is actually arguing about.

**21. Carolina Panthers.** The NFC four seed lost 34-31 to the Rams, which was the closest anyone came to Los Angeles in January outside the conference title game.

**22. Pittsburgh Steelers.** Hosted a wild card game and lost it 30-6. Michael Pittman Jr. joins DK Metcalf on a three year, 59 million dollar extension and the quarterback question is still open.

**23. Indianapolis Colts.** Started 8-2, lost seven straight to close, and Daniel Jones tore an Achilles in Week 14. He says he is cleared to do everything.

**24. Atlanta Falcons.** Fired Raheem Morris after a strong finish, hired Kevin Stefanski, extended Drake London and Kyle Pitts Sr., then signed Tua Tagovailoa for 1.3 million dollars behind Michael Penix Jr.

**25. New Orleans Saints.** [Rank only. No verified 2026 anchor.]

**26. New York Giants.** John Harbaugh takes over a roster waiting on Malik Nabers from an ACL tear and Cam Skattebo from a tibia and ankle injury.

**27. Las Vegas Raiders.** A two first round pick trade for Maxx Crosby was reversed on medical grounds, leaving Las Vegas with a disgruntled pass rusher and no settled read on his value.

**28. Tennessee Titans.** Year two for Cam Ward, with Solomon Thomas added to the interior from Dallas.

**29. Cleveland Browns.** Todd Monken inherits a Shedeur Sanders and Deshaun Watson competition, plus Jared Verse and the picks that came back for Myles Garrett.

**30. Arizona Cardinals.** 3-14, last in the NFLPA report card among all 32 teams, and 46 million dollars in dead money from cutting Kyler Murray.

**31. New York Jets.** Traded for Geno Smith, who finished 27th in QBR and led the NFL with 17 interceptions.

**32. Miami Dolphins.** Fired the coach and the general manager, cut the quarterback, and signed Malik Willis for 67.5 million dollars into a receiver room that is not settled.

# 2. NFL MVP (Offense)

The award has gone to a quarterback thirteen straight times and to a non-quarterback once since 2001. That is not a preference. It is the shape of the ballot.

## Top 15

**1. Josh Allen, Buffalo.** The only player with 3,000 passing and 500 rushing yards last season, at a higher passer rating than his MVP year. He finished third.

**2. Drake Maye, New England.** He lost by five points. New England then added A.J. Brown, Romeo Doubs and Alijah Vera-Tucker, and the schedule that flattered the record gets harder.

**3. Lamar Jackson, Baltimore.** Two wins and a second place already. The staff around him is new, which is the variable, not the quarterback.

**4. Matthew Stafford, Los Angeles.** The reigning winner returns Nacua and Adams and gains Garrett on the other side of the ball. He is 38, which is the only line in the case that matters.

**5. Joe Burrow, Cincinnati.** He is the book's largest liability because turf toe cost him ten games and nothing else about the profile changed.

**6. Patrick Mahomes, Kansas City.** The award has not gone to a quarterback returning from a December ACL tear. That sentence is the entire argument against him and there is not a second one.

**7. Justin Herbert, Los Angeles.** Two conditions. The Chargers have to clear Denver in the division, and Alt has to play sixteen games. Neither is exotic.

**8. Jordan Love, Green Bay.** Inside the market's top ten again, on a roster that returns intact and a division that has not been settled in August for years.

**9. Dak Prescott, Dallas.** The first NFC name on the board. Every Prescott case has ended at the same place, which is winning the East.

**10. Caleb Williams, Chicago.** A 27 to 7 touchdown-to-interception line in year two. The distance from efficient to voted-on is January relevance, and Chicago has not had any.

**11. Sam Darnold, Seattle.** He threw for 346 yards and three touchdowns in the NFC Championship Game and does not appear in the market's top ten. That is unusual for a quarterback who just won it.

**12. Trevor Lawrence, Jacksonville.** The board has moved toward him for the first time since the rookie contract. The roster around him moved first.

**13. Jared Goff, Detroit.** 4,564 yards, 34 touchdowns, eight interceptions, 106.5 passing EPA. He is a 35-to-1 shot because Detroit has Gibbs, not because the numbers are short.

**14. Bo Nix, Denver.** He watched the conference title game from the sideline. Denver's defense means his offense only has to be good, which historically is not how this award is won.

**15. C.J. Stroud, Houston.** Fifty-to-one, and the defense means eleven wins is reachable without a career year. The case requires the career year anyway.

## Five dark horses

**Jahmyr Gibbs, Detroit.** The shortest non-quarterback price on the board. The last non-quarterback to win did it in 2012, and he was the whole offense.

**Ja'Marr Chase, Cincinnati.** A receiving triple crown inside a twelve-win Cincinnati season is the only version of a receiver MVP voters have ever half-entertained.

**Jaxon Smith-Njigba, Seattle.** Champion, hundred-to-one, and the target share of a team that just lost its lead back. Three things that do not usually appear together.

**Jalen Hurts, Philadelphia.** New coordinator, identical personnel, and an offense with a full season of visible room. The narrative is prewritten if the numbers arrive.

**Myles Garrett, Los Angeles.** A defensive player has not won since 1986. A twenty-plus-sack season on the league's most-watched roster is the closest anyone has come to building the case since.

# 2b. NFL Defensive Player

Seeded because preseason is the only moment a defensive board costs nothing. Nobody prices this market in July, which means there is no consensus to be measured against and no reason to defer to one.

**1. Myles Garrett, Los Angeles.** Set the all time single season sack record and was then traded to a roster that lost a conference title game by four. The record was set on a team that went nowhere.

**2. Will Anderson Jr., Houston.** 85 quarterback pressures and 35 quick pressures at an 8.0 percent rate, produced while being chipped at 22.7 percent, the third highest rate in the league.

**3. Trey Hendrickson, Baltimore.** Four years and 112 million dollars, signed within days of Baltimore's trade for a different pass rusher collapsing on a physical.

**4. Maxx Crosby, Las Vegas.** Two first round picks went back the other way when the deal was reversed on medical grounds. The only rank on this board that is a question about an MRI.

**5. Nick Bosa, San Francisco.** Returning from an ACL tear on a front that lost Mykell Williams to one as well, so both edges rebuild at once.

**6. Jared Verse, Cleveland.** The centerpiece of the Garrett return, on a roster with no offensive reason to be watched.

**7. Dexter Lawrence, Cincinnati.** Cincinnati traded a top 10 pick for him, the first time the franchise has done that in the common draft era.

**8. Travon Walker, Jacksonville.** Extended this offseason on a front that produced inconsistent pressure and just lost Devin Lloyd behind it.

**9. Christian Gonzalez, New England.** A 2025 Pro Bowler who skipped most of the voluntary program in an unresolved extension standoff.

**10. Xavier Watts, Atlanta.** Half of a safety pairing with Jessie Bates III on the one Atlanta unit that did not need fixing.

## Five dark horses

**Mykell Williams, San Francisco.** ACL return on a front that needs one of its two edges to hold up.

**Boye Mafe, Cincinnati.** Arrived alongside Lawrence in the same rebuild, on the defense that has wasted three years of Burrow.

**Ernest Jones IV, Seattle.** Called the unit that held New England to 13 points in a Super Bowl.

**Avieon Terrell, Atlanta.** A second round pick into a secondary that already had three starters, opposite his brother.

**Shemar Stewart, Cincinnati.** First full NFL offseason after a rookie year that was always going to be developmental.

# 3. The Sportsvyn 25

Twenty-five teams, ranked. Not a projected playoff field: the Playoff Picture next to it is market derived and runs twelve, and two twelves on one page would be a collision. This is the poll.

Ranks 16 to 20 carry the dark horse band. Every read below is anchored to a 2025 result or a stated offseason move; where neither existed the team is not on the board, which is the whole reason four teams that will appear in the AP preseason 25 do not appear in this one.

## The board

**1. Ohio State.** An FPI of 28.7, the highest of 138 teams. Sayin completed 77 percent for more than 3,600 yards and Jeremiah Smith returns with Carnell Tate gone to the NFL.

**2. Texas.** They went 10-3 and beat the third-ranked and eighteenth-ranked teams in the closing stretch. Cam Coleman and Hollywood Smothers arrive for a quarterback whose second half was his first good football.

**3. Notre Dame.** Carr's 9.4 yards per attempt tied for second nationally and broke the program's single-season passer rating record. He is the first returning starter Notre Dame has had since 2020.

**4. Oregon.** Thirteen wins, a semifinal, and fourth in the final AP poll at 13-2. Moore came back rather than enter the draft after a 30-touchdown season, and the November date in Columbus is the whole schedule.

**5. Georgia.** 12-1 and an SEC title with a first-year starter who completed better than 70 percent and scored 31 times. Stockton is the least discussed quarterback of the top five.

**6. Indiana.** The first 16-0 team in the sport's history is replacing the Heisman winner who made it possible. Hoover inherits the system, not the season.

**7. Miami.** Second in the final AP poll at 13-3 after reaching the title game as a ten seed, now handing the offense to a transfer who threw for nearly 4,000 yards and 34 touchdowns and won the ACC with Duke.

**8. Ole Miss.** Third in the final poll at 13-2. Chambliss won the job in Week 3, threw for nearly 4,000 yards, finished eighth in Heisman voting and litigated his way to a sixth year. He also lost his head coach to LSU.

**9. Texas A&M.** Eighth in the final poll after starting 11-0 and losing to Texas and then to Miami in the first round. Reed returns, and so does the schedule that made the 11-0 possible.

**10. Texas Tech.** Twelve wins, a Big 12 title, and a 23-0 quarterfinal shutout on the way out. The two-deep is still the deepest in the conference and the quarterback room is not settled.

**11. LSU.** Unranked in the final poll. Kiffin arrives with 41 transfers in and 38 out and Leavitt at quarterback, which is the least continuous roster anywhere in this top fifteen. Week 3 against Ole Miss is the game the season is built around.

**12. Alabama.** Ninth in the final poll, which is four spots higher than a 28-7 loss in Atlanta and a 38-3 quarterfinal deserved. They beat Oklahoma 34-24 on the road and have no settled quarterback. The name is the only argument left and we do not rank names.

**13. Oklahoma.** Thirteenth in the final poll at 10-3, and Mateer returns to a roster that added through the portal. The rushing volume is the differentiator; the SEC schedule is what turns every number into a résumé line.

**14. Utah.** Fourteenth in the final poll at 11-2, closing with a 44-22 win over Nebraska in Las Vegas. The least discussed eleven-win team in the country.

**15. BYU.** Eleventh in the final poll. 11-1 in the regular season with the only loss to Texas Tech, then a second loss to Texas Tech in the Big 12 title game, then 25-21 over Georgia Tech in the Pop-Tarts Bowl. Bachmeier and LJ Martin both return.

## Dark horses, ranks 16 to 20

**16. Penn State.** Seven and six, and then most of an Iowa State roster arrived with Rocco Becht. The schedule is the softest of any team with real talent.

**17. USC.** Twentieth in the final poll with nine wins, Riley, and a roster nobody is putting inside the top fifteen. That combination has produced exactly one kind of season in his career and it has not happened yet at USC.

**18. Michigan.** Twenty-first in the final poll, closing with a 41-27 loss to Texas in the Citrus Bowl. The gap between that and a playoff berth is one quarterback.

**19. Virginia.** Sixteenth in the final poll at 11-3, with a 13-7 Gator Bowl win over Missouri. Eleven wins in the ACC and almost nobody outside Charlottesville noticed.

**20. Hawai'i.** Nine wins, Alejado back, and two Power Four road games on the schedule. Winning one of them is a full playoff argument in a conference whose best teams left.

## Ranks 21 to 25

**21. Vanderbilt.** Fifteenth in the final poll, ending on a 34-27 ReliaQuest Bowl loss to Iowa.

**22. Iowa.** Seventeenth in the final poll, and the team that won that game.

**23. Tulane.** Eighteenth in the final poll and an eleven seed in the playoff, out 41-10 to Ole Miss. Jon Sumrall then left for Florida.

**24. James Madison.** Nineteenth in the final poll and the twelve seed, out 51-34 to Oregon. The only Sun Belt team in the final 25.

**25. TCU.** Twenty-fifth in the final poll, now without Josh Hoover, who led the nation in passing and left for Indiana.

## Named and left off

Four teams that will sit inside the AP preseason 25 are absent here, on purpose. **Tennessee**, **Clemson**, **Florida** and **Auburn** all finished the 2025 season unranked. Clemson went 7-6 with a new quarterback in Vizzina. Florida and Auburn both fired their coaches. Preseason polls rank those four on history and recruiting; this board ranks the season that just happened and the roster that came out of it. When one of them earns a number in September it will enter with movement, which is more interesting than seeding it now.

# 4. Heisman

Quarterbacks have won sixteen of the last twenty. One preseason favorite in the last decade has won it. Both of those are true and they point in opposite directions, which is why this list is fifteen deep.

## Top 15

**1. Julian Sayin, Ohio State.** Seventy-seven percent, more than 3,600 yards, more than 30 touchdowns, on the highest-rated team in the sport. The market has him third and cannot explain why.

**2. Arch Manning, Texas.** 3,163 yards, 26 touchdowns, 400 on the ground and ten more scores, and a 155-yard rushing night against Michigan. Two new receivers and a rebuilt line arrive for the encore.

**3. CJ Carr, Notre Dame.** A redshirt freshman who closed on a ten-game winning streak with 24 touchdowns against six interceptions. Denbrock's third year calling it is the reason to expect more volume.

**4. Trinidad Chambliss, Ole Miss.** Division II to SEC Newcomer of the Year to eighth in the voting, in one season, starting in Week 3. He gets LSU in Week 3 and Texas in Week 8.

**5. Dante Moore, Oregon.** Thirty touchdowns and an elite completion rate, and he turned down the draft. Oregon at Ohio State in November is the single largest stage either quarterback gets.

**6. Jeremiah Smith, Ohio State.** Seventy-six catches, roughly 1,300 yards, fifteen scores, and Tate's targets now vacant. No receiver has won since 2020, which is the only thing standing in front of him.

**7. Gunner Stockton, Georgia.** Better than 70 percent and 31 total touchdowns while winning the SEC as a first-year starter. His fourth-quarter numbers against ranked teams are the case.

**8. Darian Mensah, Miami.** Nearly 4,000 yards and 34 touchdowns and an ACC title at Duke, now in an offense with more talent than the one he did it in.

**9. Marcel Reed, Texas A&M.** He took a program to its first playoff and returns everything that mattered. The schedule gives him three November chances to be the story.

**10. Josh Hoover, Indiana.** He inherits the system that produced a Heisman winner and a 16-0 season. That is either the best job in the sport or the worst, and there is no third option.

**11. Sam Leavitt, LSU.** A dual-threat starter in a Kiffin offense in Death Valley, before taking a snap. Buzz is not production, but the fit is the most obvious on this list.

**12. John Mateer, Oklahoma.** The rushing volume is the differentiator and the SEC schedule is the amplifier. Every big number he posts happens against a ranked defense.

**13. LaNorris Sellers, South Carolina.** The traits have never been the question. The record has, and Heisman voters have not given it to a team with four losses since the award existed in its current shape.

**14. Ahmad Hardy, Missouri.** The second-leading rusher in the country returns. Running backs face the same wall receivers do, and he is the one most likely to test it.

**15. Malachi Toney, Miami.** He was central to a run to the national title game as an underclassman. A new quarterback with more arm talent is the variable that makes the target share matter.

## Five dark horses

**Kewan Lacy, Ole Miss.** The back in the offense with the most watched quarterback in the sport. Volume plus attention is the only route a runner has ever had.

**Rocco Becht, Penn State.** He brought most of an offense with him from Ames to a schedule with no ranked November. Nobody has priced what that combination produces.

**Drew Mestemaker, Oklahoma State.** An Air Raid transplanted whole into a conference without defenses, run by a quarterback some evaluators have in the 2027 first round. The numbers will be absurd. The record decides whether they count.

**Micah Alejado, Hawai'i.** Mountain West freshman of the year, back with his roster held together. Two Power Four road games and a playoff-relevant conference title is a full campaign.

**Cutter Boley, Arizona State.** He replaces a quarterback who left for LSU, with two transfer receivers and a coach who has done more with less twice already.
