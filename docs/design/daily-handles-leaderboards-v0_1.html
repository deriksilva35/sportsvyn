What this is

Handles and leaderboards, mocked for markup. No build. v1.2 grammar throughout, ink. Every proposal below is argued rather than asserted, and the one thing I would call a hard finding is in THE LEAK LAW — it changes how the overall board must be computed, not just how it looks.

1 · Handle constraints — proposed

RuleProposalWhy
Length3–153 lets initials work; 15 is what fits the leaderboard row at 390px beside a score and a badge without truncating.

Automated matching produces the Scunthorpe problem and still misses; a list plus a manual override table is the same posture the nflverse name matcher already uses, and it fails toward a human rather than toward a wrong guess.
RenameOnce per 30 days, from /accountA leaderboard is a record of who did what. Free renames make last week's board unreadable and let someone swap into a name a rival just abandoned. 30 days is long enough to make that pointless and short enough to fix a regretted handle.

2 · Where it stores — proposed: a column, not a profile table

users.handle text, users.handle_changed_at timestamptz, plus CREATE UNIQUE INDEX ON users (lower(handle)) and a small handle_history(user_id, handle, claimed_at, released_at) for moderation and for refusing an instant re-claim of a just-abandoned name.

Argued against a profile table: it is one string. A separate table means a join on every leaderboard row for that string, and the uniqueness constraint — the only part that is actually hard — belongs on the same row as the identity it makes unique. If a profile grows an avatar, a bio and notification prefs later, THAT is when a table earns its join; adding it now is a join we pay for on every board render in exchange for nothing.

3 · Season points — argued, and the raw-score option is measurably wrong

I measured the ceiling of all 174 weeks in the corpus (top five PPR performances, the shape the board rewards):
minp10medianp90max
127.0153.9170.9193.9233.5
An 84% spread from the easiest week to the richest, and 40 points between p10 and p90. So SUM OF DAILY SCORES is out: it would rank partly on which days you happened to show up, and two players of identical skill would separate by dozens of points on draw luck alone.

AVERAGE PERCENT OF PERFECT fixes difficulty but breaks the product: one player with one lucky day tops a board of people who have shown up for a month, and any minimum-games threshold is an arbitrary cliff.

PROPOSAL — tier points, summed: HALL OF FAME 5 · MVP 4 · PRO BOWLER 3 · STARTER 2 · PRACTICE SQUAD 1 · DNF 0 · missed day 0. Tiebreak on cumulative percent-of-perfect, then on days played.
It normalises difficulty for free, because the tier ladder is already a RATIO of the day's perfect. It is an integer, which screenshots and reads at a glance ("41 pts, 3 HOF"). It rewards showing up, which is the entire point of a daily. And it keeps a newcomer's gap finite and legible — five points a day, not "catch up 900 raw points".

Missed days score zero, with no penalty and no exclusion, and the row shows played 12/17 so the zero is legible rather than mysterious. Excluding missed days would quietly convert the board into an average and re-open the one-lucky-day problem.

SEASON-SCOPED, not all-time. A board that never resets is unwinnable by November and stops being a reason to come back — and this product is already seasonal, so the NFL season boundary is a reset the reader expects rather than one we invented. Career HOF/MVP counts stay all-time on the profile, which is where a permanent record belongs.

4 · THE LEAK LAW — this one is a finding, not a preference

Your rule is "no other player's score for an OPEN day appears anywhere". The non-obvious consequence: the overall standings must be computed through the last CLOSED day, not through today.

If today's entry contributed to season points, then the OVERALL module on the entered screen moves the moment a rival locks — and anyone watching a total go from 38 to 42 has just learned that player took an MVP today. The leaderboard becomes a side channel for exactly the number the leak rule protects. Every board below is therefore labelled THROUGH YESTERDAY, and that label is load-bearing rather than decorative.

5 · Anonymous fallback — not "Player #id"

Player #37 publishes the user count and the signup order, and it is enumerable: watch the board for a week and you learn roughly how many people this thing has. On a product with two-digit user numbers that is a number worth not printing.

Proposal: Player 7f3a — four hex characters of HMAC(user_id, server secret). Stable across days so a rival is recognisable week to week, non-enumerable, and it leaks neither count nor join order. Shown in muted grey with no badge styling, so an unclaimed name reads as a blank rather than as a person. The claim screen exists to replace it, and the board is the argument for claiming.

Open questions for markup

1. Skip on the claim screen — I included one. A forced handle before the first entry is a wall in front of the thing they came for, and Player 7f3a is a working identity. Cut it if you want handles mandatory.
2. Podium depth on the rules screen — top 5 as specified, plus your own row pinned. The pinned row is an addition; it is also the only part a twelfth-place player cares about.
3. Streak — shown in the expanded overall. It is a fifth column on a 390px row, so on mobile it is absent. Worth deciding whether streak is a leaderboard column or a profile stat.
4. DNF display — listed unranked at the foot of today's board. Alternative is omitting them, which makes 1,204 entries and 1,180 rows disagree with no explanation.
5. Season boundary — NFL season, so the reset is early September. That means the first season is a stub of a few weeks. Start the clock at the real boundary, or run the first “season” long through to next September?
6. Handle prefix — I rendered @handle throughout. It reads as a social handle, which may be more promise than we want to make.
